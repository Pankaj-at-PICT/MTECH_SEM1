1. Research Paper Title
“A Graph-Based Heuristic for Bus Route Planning Using Taxi Origin-Destination Data”
2. Research Paper Details
Authors: Zihao Yu, Brian Godwin S. Lim, Renzo Roel P. Tan, Hirotaka Kaji, and Kazushi Ikeda.
Published in: IEEE Access, Volume 14, 2026.
DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3652636.
Main topic: Data-driven bus stop selection and bus route planning using large-scale taxi Origin-Destination (OD) data.
Case study: Susono City, Shizuoka, Japan.
3. Domain
Transportation / Public Transit / Mobility Planning

The paper belongs to the domain of data-driven public transportation and bus route planning. It uses graph theory, spatial clustering, and mobility data to design bus routes.

2. Application Covered

The proposed method is applied to Susono City, Shizuoka, Japan. The study focuses on designing practical bus routes for a mid-sized suburban aging city, where public transportation is challenging because of low-density settlements, mountainous areas, and limited transportation resources.
The application uses taxi Origin-Destination (OD) data to identify mobility hotspots and determine suitable bus stops and routes. The study ultimately generates four bus routes for Susono City.

5. Major Contribution

The paper proposes a graph-based, data-driven heuristic for complete bus route planning. Its main contributions are:

Uses taxi OD data to identify real mobility hotspots.
Uses DBSCAN to identify candidate bus stops.
Uses K-medoids to divide stops into bus routes.
Uses a Hamiltonian-path heuristic with Two-opt to construct practical routes.
Integrates pedestrian and road networks, so route planning considers real-world connectivity and physical distances.
Provides a framework that can potentially be adapted to other suburban cities.

6. Dataset

The main dataset is taxi Origin-Destination (OD) GPS data from Susono City.

Period: October 1, 2022 – April 9, 2024
Contains origin and destination GPS coordinates
Includes trip start/end timestamps
Includes trip distance
Contains anonymized vehicle and driver IDs
Contains tens of thousands of trip records
The study also uses OpenStreetMap (OSM) pedestrian and road-network data.

7. Methodology

The proposed methodology begins with the collection and preprocessing of taxi Origin-Destination (OD) data. The origin and destination points from the taxi trips are projected onto the pedestrian network so that the actual walking distance and network connectivity can be considered. The study then uses DBSCAN clustering to identify areas with high concentrations of taxi trips. These high-demand areas are considered as potential locations for bus stops.

In the next stage, the identified candidate bus stops are refined by removing redundant stops that are located very close to each other. The selected stops are then projected onto the road network so that they can be accessed by buses. K-medoids clustering is used to divide the bus stops into different groups based on the shortest road-network distances. This helps in forming suitable route segments while considering the actual road structure rather than simple geographical distance.

Finally, the researchers construct the bus routes using a Hamiltonian path-based heuristic. A Multi-Fragment heuristic is initially used to connect the selected bus stops, and the Two-opt algorithm is then applied to improve the routes by removing unnecessary detours and reducing the overall travel distance. The resulting routes are evaluated using factors such as route length, population coverage, and taxi OD coverage to determine how effectively the proposed methodology serves the transportation demand of the study area.


8. Analysis and Outcomes

The proposed methodology was tested using taxi Origin-Destination (OD) data from Susono City, Japan. The analysis resulted in the generation of four bus routes. The researchers evaluated these routes based on factors such as population coverage, taxi OD coverage, and total route distance. The results showed that the proposed approach was able to identify routes that connected important areas of the city.

The analysis showed that the proposed bus network could cover more than 93% of the population within a 500 m walking distance of the planned routes. In addition, the proposed method achieved 85.9% coverage of taxi OD data, whereas the baseline method achieved 67.9%. This indicates that the proposed approach was able to capture a larger portion of the observed transportation demand.

The study also compared the computational performance of the proposed approach with the baseline method. The proposed algorithm required approximately 230 seconds, while the baseline required approximately 390 seconds in the reported experiment. Overall, the analysis demonstrates that combining real taxi mobility data with clustering and graph-based route optimization can produce practical bus routes with good demand and population coverage

9. Conclusion Drawn

The paper concludes that the proposed graph-based heuristic approach is effective for planning bus routes using real-world taxi mobility data. By combining DBSCAN, K-medoids, Hamiltonian path construction, and Two-opt optimization, the method can identify suitable bus stops and construct routes according to actual transportation demand.

The results from the Susono City case study show that the generated routes provide high population coverage and taxi OD coverage. Therefore, the study demonstrates that integrating mobility data with road and pedestrian network information can support more systematic and demand-oriented bus route planning.

Overall, the proposed framework provides a practical approach for public transportation planning in suburban and aging cities. The authors also indicate that the methodology can potentially be adapted to other areas with similar transportation-planning challenges.

10. Limitations

The study has some limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. First, the proposed methodology is evaluated using data from Susono City, Japan, so the results are based on one specific geographic and transportation context. The effectiveness of the approach in other cities may require further validation.

Another limitation is that the study mainly relies on taxi OD data to identify transportation demand. Taxi trips may not completely represent the travel patterns of all residents because different groups of people may use taxis, buses, private vehicles, or other modes differently.

The methodology also involves several parameters and algorithmic choices, such as the DBSCAN parameters, bus-stop selection radius, number of clusters (K), and route optimization settings. Changes in these parameters may affect the resulting bus routes and their coverage.

11. Future Scope

The proposed methodology can be extended to other cities and regions to evaluate its general applicability. Future studies can use mobility data from different transportation modes, not only taxi data, to obtain a more comprehensive understanding of travel demand.

The approach can also be improved by considering additional factors such as traffic conditions, bus operating costs, passenger demand at different times, and changes in travel patterns. Further research can evaluate the proposed routes using real-world bus operations and passenger feedback.

12. Research Questions Answered

Research Question 1: How can suitable bus-stop locations be identified based on actual transportation demand?
Solution/Strategy: The paper uses taxi Origin-Destination (OD) data to identify areas with high travel demand. DBSCAN clustering is applied to group nearby demand points and identify potential bus-stop locations. Redundant stops are then removed based on a specified distance threshold.

Research Question 2: How can the selected bus stops be grouped into suitable bus routes?
Solution/Strategy: The paper applies K-medoids clustering to divide the selected bus stops into different route groups. Instead of using simple geographical distance, the method uses shortest-path distances on the road network, making the grouping more suitable for actual bus movement.

Research Question 3: How can efficient routes be constructed between the selected bus stops?
Solution/Strategy: The paper formulates the route construction as a Hamiltonian path problem. A Multi-Fragment heuristic is first used to construct the routes, followed by the Two-opt algorithm to remove unnecessary detours and improve route efficiency.

Research Question 4: Can the proposed strategy provide adequate transportation coverage?
Solution/Strategy: The generated routes are evaluated using population coverage and taxi OD coverage. The proposed approach achieved high coverage in the Susono City case study, demonstrating that the combination of OD-data analysis, clustering, and graph-based route optimization can be used for data-driven bus route planning.
